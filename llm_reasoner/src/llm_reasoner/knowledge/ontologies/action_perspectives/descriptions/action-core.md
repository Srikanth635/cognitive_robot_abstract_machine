The action core describes an action as a frame: the slots its participants fill, such as
the agent, the source, the substance and the goal, and the parts of them that matter,
such as the source's lip or the goal's opening; what the action is for, as the state it
aims at and the slots that state concerns; what it brings about, as the slots it changes
and the states they end in; and the choices it leaves open, each a kind of quantity about
a slot, possibly measured relative to another slot: for pouring, such as how far the
source is tilted, where its lip is relative to the goal, how high above the goal it is
held, and how much is poured.

The core is about kinds of action, never about one world: it names no object, place or
robot.
