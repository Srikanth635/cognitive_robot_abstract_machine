The image schemas describe the spatial and force structure underneath an action, as Johnson,
Lakoff and Talmy describe it: the recurring patterns of moving along a path, of containing and
being contained, of being above or below, of supporting and touching, that a person grasps
about an action before any detail of it.

Its vocabulary lets a knowledge base say, for an action's frame, which schemas the action
instantiates, such as source-path-goal, containment, verticality, support and contact, and for
each of them:

- which of the core's slots take part and in which role of the schema, such as the source's lip
  as where the path starts and the goal's opening as where it ends and the way in;
- what the schema requires of those slots in relation to each other, such as the source's lip
  being above the goal's opening, or the substance being contained in the source before the
  action and in the goal after it;
- which of the core's parameter dimensions the relation constrains, such as the position of the
  source's lip relative to the goal and its height above the goal deciding whether the lip is
  over the opening;
- which quantities decide whether the relation holds, such as the centre and the size of the
  opening and the position of the lip, without giving any of them a value.

A schema is stated about kinds of action, never about one world: it names no object, place or
robot.
