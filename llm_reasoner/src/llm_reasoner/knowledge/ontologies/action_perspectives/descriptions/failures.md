The failures describe how an action can go wrong and how it is put right, in terms of the
action core's frame: which slot or relation between slots fails, how a person or a robot
would notice, which of the core's parameter dimensions caused it, and which change to those
dimensions corrects it.

Its vocabulary lets a knowledge base say, for an action's frame, for each failure:

- which slot or part of a slot it concerns, such as the source's lip or the goal's opening;
- what goes wrong, stated as a state of the world a person can observe, such as the
  substance landing beside the goal's opening rather than in it, the source tilted too little
  for the substance to flow, or the agent unable to move the source to the pose the action
  needs;
- how it is noticed, as the quantity that shows it, such as how far the lip ends from the
  opening's centre, the tilt at which the substance starts to flow, or whether the motion to
  the pose finishes;
- which of the core's parameter dimensions it comes from, such as the lip's position relative
  to the goal, the source's orientation, or the height above the goal;
- the correction, stated as a change to those dimensions, in which direction and for what
  reason, such as moving the lip's position towards the opening's centre, tilting the source
  further, or choosing a different orientation the agent can reach, and not only a change to
  the tilt;
- whether the failure can be corrected during the action or only by trying again.

The vocabulary is about kinds of action, never about one world: it names no object, place or
robot.
