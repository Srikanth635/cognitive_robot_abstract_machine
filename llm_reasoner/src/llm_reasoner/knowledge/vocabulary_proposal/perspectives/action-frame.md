The action frame describes an action as FrameNet and Minsky's frames do: the participants it
needs, what each of them is normally like, the defaults that hold when nothing says
otherwise, what must hold before the action, and what it changes.

Its vocabulary lets a knowledge base say, for each slot of the action core's frame:

- what kind of thing normally fills it, such as a container for the source of a pour;
- what the filler is expected to have or to afford, such as an opening, the ability to hold
  a substance, or standing upright, stated as a condition a world can check about the
  filler rather than as the class the filler belongs to;
- the default that holds when nothing says otherwise, such as pouring until the goal holds
  the wanted amount rather than until the source is empty;

and, for the action as a whole:

- what must hold before it starts, such as the agent holding the source;
- what it changes beyond the core's consequences, such as the source becoming lighter;
- why each parameter dimension the core names matters to the action, in terms of the
  slots it concerns, such as the position of the source's lip relative to the goal's
  opening deciding whether the substance lands in the goal.

The vocabulary is about kinds of action, never about one world: it names no object,
place or robot.
